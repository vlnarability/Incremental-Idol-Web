   227→   227→3. Feature points I'd prioritize
   228→   228→
   229→   229→A. Idols with distinct identities
   230→   230→
   231→   231→Idols have traits such as charisma, work ethic, musical talent, controversy, and fan loyalty. Different combinations create different strategies.
   232→   232→
   233→   233→A technically mediocre idol with exceptional charisma might outperform a virtuoso with terrible marketing. Certain traits should synergize with genres, teams, or audiences.
   234→   234→
   235→   235→B. Trends and the fame lifecycle
   236→   236→
   237→   237→Genres, aesthetics, and formats rise and fall. A trendsetter can generate enormous returns, but copying a trend after it peaks wastes resources.
   238→   238→
   239→   239→This creates interesting decisions beyond purchasing the next upgrade.
   240→   240→
   241→   241→C. Fans are not all equal
   242→   242→
   243→   243→Separate casual listeners, dedicated fans, superfans, and cultural evangelists.
   244→   244→
   245→   245→Casual fans provide reach; dedicated fans provide reliable revenue; superfans generate disproportionate spending and word-of-mouth; evangelists help expand into new markets.
   246→   246→
   247→   247→D. Automation as progression
   248→   248→
   249→   249→Hire assistants to automate clicking, producers to automate releases, managers to automate idols, executives to automate agencies, and AI systems to automate the conglomerate.
   250→   250→
   251→   251→Every major stage should make the previous stage feel like a machine you've built and can now rely on.
   252→   252→
   253→   253→E. Permanent prestige upgrades
   254→   254→
   255→   255→Instead of resetting everything, award a permanent meta-currency such as Legacy or Influence.
   256→   256→
   257→   257→Spend it on faster early progression, additional starting resources, new idol archetypes, exclusive mechanics, or more efficient automation. Major prestige tiers could also unlock entirely new systems.
   258→   258→
   259→   259→F. Audiences beyond your current scale
   260→   260→
   261→   261→Earth has countries and cultures. The galaxy has species and civilizations. The universe has realities with different laws of physics.
   262→   262→
   263→   263→Each expansion should introduce a new constraint rather than simply adding another zero to the fan count.

   280→   280→Fame saturation
   281→   281→
   282→   282→There is a natural problem with the premise: if everyone on Earth is already your fan, where does growth come from?
   283→   283→
   284→   284→Make saturation a central mechanic. When your market approaches 100% penetration, fan acquisition slows dramatically. You must innovate, deepen loyalty, expand into new audiences, or prestige.
   285→   285→
   286→   286→Later, the same problem repeats at larger scales. You dominate Earth, but not Mars. You dominate the galaxy, but not alternate universes.

   296→   296→
   297→   297→A scandal might multiply attention while damaging approval. Depending on the strategy, that could be disastrous or profitable. A management agency might even intentionally cultivate controversy, at the risk of long-term fan loyalty.

   585→   585→How the transitions should work
   586→   586→
   587→   587→Each prestige should have four components:
   588→   588→
   589→   589→    A capstone challenge: Prove that you've mastered the current era.
   590→   590→
   591→   591→    A transformation event: Sell, delegate, transcend, or repurpose the existing operation.
   592→   592→
   593→   593→    A permanent legacy reward: Retain something valuable from the previous run.
   594→   594→
   595→   595→    A new management problem: Introduce a mechanic that could not meaningfully exist in the previous era.
   596→   596→
   597→   597→For example, the Idol-to-Manager transition could let the player recruit the original idol as their first contracted superstar. The Manager-to-Agency transition could turn the roster into an established division. The Agency-to-Conglomerate transition could preserve its labels and talent as subsidiaries.
   598→   598→
   599→   599→This continuity makes prestige feel like the evolution of one enterprise, not a series of unrelated games.

  1025→  1025→

  1029→  1029→

  1233→  1233→Architectural rules
  1234→  1234→
  1235→  1235→    One authoritative game state. The interface displays state and submits commands; it does not calculate production independently.
  1236→  1236→
  1237→  1237→    Deterministic simulation. Given the same state, inputs, and elapsed simulation time, the economy should produce the same result.
  1238→  1238→
  1239→  1239→    Data-driven content. Upgrades, idols, gigs, contracts, and milestones should be defined as data resources rather than hardcoded UI logic.
  1240→  1240→
  1241→  1241→    Explicit prestige transitions. A dedicated service determines what is reset, converted, retained, and unlocked.
  1242→  1242→
  1243→  1243→    Versioned persistence. Saves must remain readable across game updates, with migrations for changed schemas.
  1244→  1244→
  1245→  1245→    Bounded calculations. Offline progression must not run an unbounded loop for every elapsed second.
  1246→  1246→
  1247→  1247→Avoid adding a database server, account system, cloud backend, or live-service infrastructure. They are unnecessary for the first release. Steam Cloud can be added through the platform's supported save synchronization once local saves are reliable.

  1392→  1392→

  1431→  1431→4. Incremental economy formulas
  1432→  1432→
  1433→  1433→The game needs a consistent mathematical model that scales from one performer to an agency without turning every value into an arbitrary multiplier.
  1434→  1434→A. Upgrade cost
  1435→  1435→
  1436→  1436→Use exponential pricing:
  1437→  1437→C(L)=C0rL
  1438→  1438→C(L)=C0​rL
  1439→  1439→
  1440→  1440→Where:
  1441→  1441→
  1442→  1442→    C0
  1443→  1443→    C0​ is the base cost.
  1444→  1444→
  1445→  1445→    r
  1446→  1446→    r is the cost growth factor.
  1447→  1447→
  1448→  1448→    L
  1449→  1449→    L is the current upgrade level.
  1450→  1450→
  1451→  1451→For example, with a base cost of 25 and growth factor of 1.15, each successive level costs 15% more than the previous one.
  1452→  1452→
  1453→  1453→The cumulative cost of buying n
  1454→  1454→n levels is:
  1455→  1455→Ctotal=C0rL+n−rLr−1
  1456→  1456→Ctotal​=C0​r−1rL+n−rL​
  1457→  1457→
  1458→  1458→This allows the interface to calculate the cost of buying one, ten, or all affordable levels without looping through every purchase.
  1459→  1459→B. Active clicking
  1460→  1460→
  1461→  1461→Let the value of an active performance action be:
  1462→  1462→P=P0(1+aL)(1+bS)
  1463→  1463→P=P0​(1+aL)(1+bS)
  1464→  1464→
  1465→  1465→Where P0
  1466→  1466→P0​ is the base performance value, L
  1467→  1467→L is the relevant upgrade level, S
  1468→  1468→S is a performer skill value, and a,b
  1469→  1469→a,b control the strength of those upgrades.
  1470→  1470→
  1471→  1471→Each click can generate a mixture of fans and cash:
  1472→  1472→ΔF=P⋅mF
  1473→  1473→ΔF=P⋅mF​
  1474→  1474→Δ$=P⋅m$
  1475→  1475→Δ$=P⋅m$​
  1476→  1476→
  1477→  1477→The multipliers should differ by action. A fan-interaction action might prioritize audience growth, while a merchandise action prioritizes cash.
  1478→  1478→C. Passive production
  1479→  1479→
  1480→  1480→For an automated source i
  1481→  1481→i:
  1482→  1482→Ri=Bi⋅Qi⋅Ai⋅Mi
  1483→  1483→Ri​=Bi​⋅Qi​⋅Ai​⋅Mi​
  1484→  1484→
  1485→  1485→Where:
  1486→  1486→
  1487→  1487→    Bi
  1488→  1488→    Bi​: base production rate.
  1489→  1489→
  1490→  1490→    Qi
  1491→  1491→    Qi​: quality or skill factor.
  1492→  1492→
  1493→  1493→    Ai
  1494→  1494→    Ai​: audience or market fit.
  1495→  1495→
  1496→  1496→    Mi
  1497→  1497→    Mi​: combined upgrade and synergy multiplier.
  1498→  1498→
  1499→  1499→Total production is:
  1500→  1500→Rtotal=∑i=1NRi
  1501→  1501→Rtotal​=i=1∑N​Ri​
  1502→  1502→
  1503→  1503→Keep the source-specific components visible in the interface. If an idol produces 2,400 fans per minute, the player should be able to inspect how much comes from their skill, assigned staff, genre fit, and upgrades.
  1504→  1504→D. Audience growth and saturation
  1505→  1505→
  1506→  1506→A simple model is to let fan acquisition slow as the player approaches the available audience.
  1507→  1507→dFdt=RF(1−FK)
  1508→  1508→dtdF​=RF​(1−KF​)
  1509→  1509→
  1510→  1510→Where K
  1511→  1511→K is the current addressable audience.
  1512→  1512→
  1513→  1513→This is a logistic growth model. It avoids unlimited linear fan growth within a fixed market, and makes expansion, new releases, and market diversification strategically useful.
  1514→  1514→
  1515→  1515→For a discrete simulation step, an approximation is:
  1516→  1516→ΔF=RFΔt⋅max⁡(0,1−FK)
  1517→  1517→ΔF=RF​Δt⋅max(0,1−KF​)
  1518→  1518→
  1519→  1519→Use the continuous solution or a sufficiently small step if accuracy near saturation matters. The approximation is suitable for an initial prototype, but it should be tested for large time steps.
  1520→  1520→E. Song releases
  1521→  1521→
  1522→  1522→A release should create a temporary growth opportunity rather than provide permanent linear income.
  1523→  1523→
  1524→  1524→One possible model:
  1525→  1525→Rsong(t)=R0QGexp⁡(−tτ)
  1526→  1526→Rsong​(t)=R0​QGexp(−τt​)
  1527→  1527→
  1528→  1528→Where:
  1529→  1529→
  1530→  1530→    R0
  1531→  1531→    R0​ is the base release reach.
  1532→  1532→
  1533→  1533→    Q
  1534→  1534→    Q is song quality.
  1535→  1535→
  1536→  1536→    G
  1537→  1537→    G is genre-market fit.
  1538→  1538→
  1539→  1539→    t
  1540→  1540→    t is time since release.
  1541→  1541→
  1542→  1542→    τ
  1543→  1543→    τ controls the release's decay rate.
  1544→  1544→
  1545→  1545→Promotional campaigns can temporarily increase R0
  1546→  1546→R0​ or τ
  1547→  1547→τ, while a trend can increase G
  1548→  1548→G. This makes release timing and marketing choices matter.
  1549→  1549→F. Idol and manager economics
  1550→  1550→
  1551→  1551→For each idol, calculate a gross revenue rate and an upkeep rate:
  1552→  1552→Πi=Ri−Ci
  1553→  1553→Πi​=Ri​−Ci​
  1554→  1554→
  1555→  1555→Where Ri
  1556→  1556→Ri​ is revenue and Ci
  1557→  1557→Ci​ is the cost of staff, contracts, training, and maintenance.
  1558→  1558→
  1559→  1559→In the Manager era, the player's decision is no longer simply which upgrade to buy. It becomes which idols deserve investment, which are profitable, and which are worth keeping for long-term strategic reasons.
  1560→  1560→G. Agency economics
  1561→  1561→
  1562→  1562→For the Agency era, introduce departmental overhead and shared infrastructure:
  1563→  1563→Πagency=∑i=1NΠi+Bsynergy−Coverhead−Cinvestment
  1564→  1564→Πagency​=i=1∑N​Πi​+Bsynergy​−Coverhead​−Cinvestment​
  1565→  1565→
  1566→  1566→Here, Bsynergy
  1567→  1567→Bsynergy​ represents additional profit from coordinated labels, marketing, production, and talent.
  1568→  1568→
  1569→  1569→This creates a meaningful portfolio problem: an underperforming division may be worth retaining if it boosts the entire agency, while excessive expansion can create unsustainable overhead.
  1570→  1570→H. Prestige rewards
  1571→  1571→
  1572→  1572→Prestige should reward both scale and demonstrated milestones.
  1573→  1573→
  1574→  1574→A candidate formula is:
  1575→  1575→Learned=⌊αlog⁡10(1+VV0)+βM⌋
  1576→  1576→Learned​=⌊αlog10​(1+V0​V​)+βM⌋
  1577→  1577→
  1578→  1578→Where:
  1579→  1579→
  1580→  1580→    V
  1581→  1581→    V is the current era's prestige valuation.
  1582→  1582→
  1583→  1583→    V0
  1584→  1584→    V0​ is a balancing constant.
  1585→  1585→
  1586→  1586→    M
  1587→  1587→    M is a milestone score.
  1588→  1588→
  1589→  1589→    α,β
  1590→  1590→    α,β control the relative contribution of scale and achievements.
  1591→  1591→
  1592→  1592→The logarithm prevents prestige rewards from increasing linearly with enormous late-game numbers. Milestones ensure that a player who completes meaningful challenges is rewarded even if they choose a less profitable strategy.
  1593→  1593→
  1594→  1594→Treat this as a starting model. The constants must be tuned using playtest data, and the formula should include sensible caps or minimums where needed.
  1595→  1595→5. Offline progression
  1596→  1596→
  1597→  1597→Offline production is a core feature, not an optional polish item. It needs to be predictable, configurable, and resistant to exploits.
  1598→  1598→Recommended behavior
  1599→  1599→
  1600→  1600→    Default offline cap: 8 hours.
  1601→  1601→
  1602→  1602→    Player-selectable cap: 1, 4, 8, or 12 hours.
  1603→  1603→
  1604→  1604→    Offline efficiency: initially 75% of normal passive production.
  1605→  1605→
  1606→  1606→    No offline clicks or active-only bonuses.
  1607→  1607→
